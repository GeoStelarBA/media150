Reading 2: Brain on ChatGPT and Brain Experts Analysis

ChatGPT and other Large Language Models (LLMs) are AI (Artificial Intelligence) systems that gather information across the internet to perform or automate tasks for people. Due to the nature of these AI, it has been a growing concern that people are becoming more and more reliant on them for their tasks. The article “Your Brain on ChatGPT” informs readers of studies that were performed to observe the effects of LLMs, ChatGPT in this case, on the brain by taking note of brain activity of groups of participants. These participants were tasked with writing an essay in an educational environment and they were split into three groups; the first group were instructed to use an LLM to assist them, the second group were instructed to use search engines, and the third group was told to use no tools to help. To summarize the results of the findings, cognitive engagement was evidently affected by the amount of support present between the groups with LLM assistance displaying the least engagement. When the prospect of AI initially circulated, I was against relying on it to help me with tasks due to my preference of searching the internet for information myself or with other people.

I had already made the assumption that a situation like this would be apparent, however not to this degree. As time passed, I had become more lenient on the idea of AI but only as a tool to reinforce rather than replace effort. Despite what I may have believed after reading this article, it only takes into account one type of task compared to general use or for other specific tasks. In that regard, I had also listened to “Brain Experts WARNING: Watch This Before Using ChatGPT Again!” which is an episode from The Diary of a CEO podcast; it addresses not just the presence and effect of LLMs such as ChatGPT, but also discusses the importance of the human need to struggle to improve. A major point I acknowledge and agree with is that despite how scary the impact of AI on the cognitive effort needed on a day-to-day basis, just like with most other things in society, regulation is key. Using AI isn’t inherently a bad thing just because it makes things easier, but you shouldn’t rely on it to do everything since you need to actively have your brain working to keep it in good condition. In fact, the podcast episode addresses what healthy AI usage looks like with an example of a woman interacting with ChatGPT as if it were a person. It was discussed that the significance of doing this is to allow the social part of a person’s brain to be at work while interacting with AI, leading to less exhaustion and an overall better experience.
